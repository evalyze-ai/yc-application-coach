# How partners read an application

Use this to explain to a founder why the advice is what it is. Statements carry a date and a label. VERIFIED means checked against the original or two independent sources during the October 2026 research for this skill. REPORTED means it came from a research guide or secondary summary and wasn't re-opened, so don't present it as a quote. Older process details describe how YC worked then.

## Who reads it

- **Partners decide who gets interviewed.** YC partners read applications and watch founder videos, with software helping sort the pile. Each partner can take only a limited number of companies per batch. VERIFIED in substance (YC's apply page and FAQ; multiple partner interviews).
- **Volume is very high and acceptance is around 1% or below.** In May 2024 Garry Tan said the then-latest batch was picked from about 27,000 applications, with cohorts around 250 companies (TechCrunch, May 22, 2024). YC no longer publishes per-batch acceptance rates; treat any precise rate from a third-party site as an estimate. VERIFIED for the 2024 figure.
- **One partner who wants you is enough.** Partners have described a claiming system where the partner who wants a company interviews it, and a single strong yes can carry a company. Tom Blomfield described this on 20VC (May 2024). REPORTED (from a transcript cited in research; not re-opened). The practical point is solid either way: aim to make one partner excited, not everyone mildly approving.
- **Edits after a read may never be seen.** Paul Graham and Sam Altman both said years ago that partners don't re-read applications they've already read, and today's FAQ says applications can't be edited continuously and points to an update form for material changes. VERIFIED for the current FAQ.
- **Old applications are kept.** You reference past applications from your account but fill out a new one each time (YC FAQ). VERIFIED.

## The questions in the reader's head

Dalton Caldwell, a longtime YC partner who ran admissions and is now Partner Emeritus, gave a Startup School talk in 2023 ("How to Apply and Succeed at YC") that YC's interview guide still recommends. Summaries of the talk, cross-checked across several sources:

- The reader asks: who are the founders, what's their backstory, how do they know each other, who does what; what is the idea, do I understand it, have I heard it before; what exists today and does anyone use it; what evidence is there people want it; are these founders serious; is there something impressive or special here.
- He tries to tell himself a short story about the company: characters, where it started, where it's going. Applications that make the story easy to assemble do their job.
- Claims should be backed by evidence; extraordinary claims need extraordinary evidence.
- The biggest variable for whether a team is selected for interview is the quality and quantity of technical talent on the founding team. He described the hardest position as a team with no technical founder working on an unlaunched idea in a crowded "tarpit" category. His suggested fix is adding technical talent, for example via YC Co-Founder Matching. VERIFIED (quoted in an independent summary of the transcript; matches several talk summaries).
- Be wary of outside parties claiming they can influence admissions. VERIFIED (talk summaries).
- Interviews are 10-minute Zoom calls; partners expect you to know your application. VERIFIED.

## More statements worth knowing

- **Domain expertise can be built.** YC's Jon Xu (June 2026 lesson on picking ideas) treats domain expertise as something founders develop through concentrated customer work, and warns that splitting attention across many ideas gives misleading feedback. His test is practical: can you explain how the customer's business runs, its recurring crises, why this problem matters to them, and what a fix would be worth? Use that test on the "domain expertise" part of the idea answer: replace claims of passion with what you observed, the belief it changed, what you shipped and what users did next. REPORTED (research guide read the transcript; not re-opened here).
- **Software assists, people decide.** A 2021 YC post on the admissions team described human judgment assisted by custom software in a process that keeps changing. That's historical context, not a description of how a 2026 application is screened. REPORTED.
- **The one network signal YC has named.** Sam Altman's May 2015 post "One surprising hack to get into YC" said that if you've worked at a YC company and get a good recommendation from its founder, YC gives your application extra consideration, while noting most funded founders are unknown to YC and never worked at a YC company. YC's FAQ today says you don't need to know anyone. VERIFIED for the 2015 post; it's old, so don't present it as current policy without checking. Never suggest collecting recommendations from YC founders who don't know the team.
- **Someone in the interview already wants you.** A former partner said in a 2024 webinar that at least one person in your interview has read your application closely and voted to invite you, and Paul Graham said in 2012 that interviews start with the questions readers had after reading it. So the written answers set up the interview questions. REPORTED.
- **Partners go to the weak point.** Victoria Krauchunas said in a 2026 webinar that after an opening basic question, partners spend the time on whatever worried them most, which can mean all ten minutes on go-to-market. REPORTED.
- **The interview isn't adversarial.** Dalton Caldwell's 2023 talk: the people interviewing you are likely to be your partners if you get in, and they're asking whether they could work well with you. Memorized speeches and not listening hurt; coaching that hides weak spots backfires. VERIFIED in substance (talk summaries).

## What partners have said they weigh

There's no public rubric, and partners weigh things differently. The most useful statements on record:

| Who, when | What they said | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Paul Graham, How to Apply essay (still on YC's site) | Be exceptionally clear and concise; give it right in the first sentence; the best answers are the most specific and the most matter-of-fact; for achievements, magnitude matters more than type; they look for people who like to beat the system; the other-ideas question can matter | VERIFIED |
| Michael Seibel, 2017 HN AMA | Top things: clear communication of what the team is doing, technical talent to build version one, moving fast without waiting for YC, a prior relationship between founders, a non-obvious insight | REPORTED |
| Dalton Caldwell, 2023 talk | Technical talent is the biggest variable for getting an interview; misleading answers get rejected; evidence for claims | VERIFIED |
| Garry Tan, 2019 tips (YC library) | Show, don't tell; start with a specific user and problem; no buzzwords; conclusion first (inverted pyramid) | REPORTED (well known; not re-opened) |
| Jessica Livingston, Lenny's Podcast, June 2024 | What founders have built before is a strong predictor; YC's software flags things like a huge equity gap between founders, founders who haven't quit or won't move, and companies mostly owned by outsiders, as prompts for attention rather than automatic rejections | REPORTED |
| Tom Blomfield, X post, January 2026 (he was a YC partner then; one source says he left in July 2026) | A personal mental model weighting the team far above the idea and the idea above traction, while noting most accepted teams have no revenue | REPORTED (X couldn't be opened) |
| YC FAQ, October 2026 | About 40% of companies funded each batch are just an idea and most have no revenue; 7% of recent batches had over $50K monthly revenue at acceptance; the team must be able to build the product | VERIFIED |

Read together: the team comes first, then sharp understanding of a real problem, then evidence of speed. Traction mostly counts as proof of the other two.

## What changed recently

- **Four batches a year** (Winter, Spring, Summer, Fall) since 2025, each with its own deadline. VERIFIED.
- **AI-written prose is visible in aggregate.** In early August 2026 partner Pete Koomen posted that applications containing "wedge" went from under 1% in Spring 2025 to over 20% in Summer 2026, and that em dashes appeared in over half of applications by Summer 2025. Partner Tyler Bosmeny noted applications got about 60% longer over three years. Reported by Business Insider and Entrepreneur (Aug 6 and 7, 2026). No partner said AI-sounding prose is automatically penalized, but plain writing in the founder's voice stands out more than before. VERIFIED.
- **The bar for what's built is rising.** With coding agents, partners expect more working product from technical teams before the interview. YC added an optional coding agent session upload for Spring 2026 and launched Paxel, an optional builder report. VERIFIED for the upload and Paxel; the "rising bar" framing is REPORTED from partner podcasts.
- **Solo founders are common.** YC said in September 2026 that nearly one in five of its companies has a solo founder. VERIFIED (YC Lightcone episode and post, multiple reports).
- **Physical and hard tech are a bigger share.** YC's September 2026 Lightcone episode described startups moving "from bits to atoms." The Winter 2027 Requests for Startups include robots made in America, the data center supply chain and space access. VERIFIED.

## Why good applications still get rejected

- **Capacity.** YC's "why not" page says there often isn't a specific reason: the median application is pretty good, YC interviews enough strong teams to fill capacity, and many good applications go unselected. VERIFIED.
- **Nothing stands out.** Paul Graham wrote long ago that most rejections at the application stage happen because nothing stood out, not because something was wrong. REPORTED (historical).
- **Small negatives add up.** One yellow flag (a single founder, cofounders not quitting jobs, still in school) is survivable; several together tip toward no. REPORTED (Paul Graham, historical).
- **Different partners, different lenses.** Founders who applied many times note that different partners index on different things, which is one reason reapplying works. REPORTED.

## Feedback

- YC gives feedback only to teams invited to interview (FAQ). VERIFIED.
- After an interview, the rejection email usually names the main concern. Founders who shared theirs most often report unclear differentiation or a crowded market, doubts about how big the company can get, or low traction. REPORTED (founder accounts).
- Some founders report a "top 10%" line in no-interview rejections. YC hasn't explained what it means beyond being close. REPORTED.
