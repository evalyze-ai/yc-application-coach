# Worked examples and editing exercises

Use these to show a founder what the rewrite pattern looks like. The home-care company is invented, for teaching only; none of its numbers are real or reusable. When rewriting a real founder's answer, use only their facts.

## A full before-and-after (invented company)

**One-liner**
- Before: "Revolutionizing care coordination with AI-powered workflows" (59 characters, no buyer, no job)
- After: "Scheduling and payroll for home-care agencies" (45 characters)

**What are you making?**
- Before: "We are building an AI-native platform that empowers home-care agencies to streamline operations, unlock efficiency and deliver better outcomes for patients and caregivers alike. Our vision is to become the operating system for care."
- After: "Home-care agencies schedule hundreds of caregiver visits a week by phone and spreadsheet, and payroll takes their office manager two days every two weeks. Our software builds the weekly schedule from caregiver availability and client needs, sends shifts by text, and turns completed visits into payroll. 14 agencies use it, scheduling about 3,100 visits a week."

**How far along are you?**
- Before: "We have strong early traction and are about to launch our payroll module, which we expect will drive significant growth."
- After: "Started in March, both full-time since May. Launched scheduling on June 2. 14 agencies use it weekly; 9 pay $400 a month ($3,600 MRR in September, up from $1,200 in July). Biggest customer: a 60-caregiver agency in Ohio. Payroll module in beta with 3 agencies, shipping to all on Oct 30."

**Competitors**
- Before: "There are some legacy players, but none of them offer our AI-first approach, and we have no direct competitors."
- After: "Most agencies use spreadsheets and phone calls. The rest use large home-health suites built for Medicare-certified agencies, priced per patient and taking months to set up. Private-pay agencies under 100 caregivers don't need the clinical features and can't afford the rollout. What we learned from 60 agency interviews: the office manager, not the owner, chooses the tool, and she'll switch for anything that saves her payroll weekend. 6 of our 14 agencies switched from a suite."

What changed: every "after" leads with the plain fact, uses dated numbers, names the real alternative, and gives one insight learned from users. None of it is clever.

## Rewrite patterns (structures to fill with real facts)

| Weak answer | Structure to replace it |
|---|---|
| "We are building the leading AI platform that transforms how businesses operate." | "[Company] helps [specific role] complete [specific task]. The user provides [input] and gets [usable result]. Today [working scope]; [planned scope] isn't built yet." |
| "We have strong traction and users love the product." | "As of [date], [defined users] completed [qualifying action] during [period]. [Cohort] came back for [natural usage occasion]. [Payment status]. The main limit of this evidence is [limit]." |
| "Our founders have backgrounds at impressive companies and are uniquely positioned to win." | "[Founder] personally built [system] for [context] and learned [constraint]. [Founder] did [complementary work]. Together we've shipped [shared work]. That's how we found [insight]." |
| "There are no real competitors. We are easier to use and have better AI." | "Our first users rely on [actual alternative]. It works for [case], but [constraint] matters for [first segment]. We change [mechanism]. Evidence so far: [behavior]. We're weaker on [honest tradeoff]." |
| "The industry is enormous, so a tiny share makes this huge." | "The first buyer is [definition]. There are about [count] ([dated source]). At [tested or assumed price], that's [calculation]. Expansion depends on [mechanism], which is [observed / a hypothesis]." |
| "We pivoted because the new market is much bigger." | "We believed [hypothesis]. After [work], we saw [result]. So we changed [customer / task / approach], kept [learning or asset], and since then [dated evidence]. We still need to test [uncertainty]." |
| "We built everything with AI, so technical execution isn't a problem." | "[Founder] owns [core system]. We use [tools] for [purpose]; the founders decide [architecture], check [quality] and handle [maintenance and failures]. The current technical bottleneck is [issue]." |
| "There are no major risks because the market is ready." | "The main open question is [risk]. What we know so far: [evidence]. We're testing it by [work]. If [result], we'd change [decision]." |

Keep the final answer natural. These are tools for thinking; don't make every answer follow the same rhythm, and a plain factual field may need only the fact.

## Exercise A: recover the facts before improving the sentence

Start from: "Our intelligent infrastructure platform empowers modern organizations to unlock scalable operational excellence."

Don't polish it. Ask the founder:
- What does someone actually receive or use?
- Which person uses it, in which situation?
- What goes in, and what useful thing comes out?
- What works today?
- What would a reader wrongly assume if the sentence stayed this broad?

Only then draft: "We make [product] for [user] to [task]. Today it [current capability]." It succeeds when the sentence rules out plausible wrong readings, not just when it gets shorter.

## Exercise B: pick the founder achievement without inventing a persona

Have each founder list real accomplishments across work, research, building, competitions, family responsibility and community work. For each: what they personally did, the obstacle, the result, the proof. Choose the one with the strongest defensible substance, even if it's outside tech. Paul Graham's essay says magnitude matters more than type.

> I [specific action] under [constraint], resulting in [result]. My part was [scope].

Use a number only when it exists and matters. Don't inflate a team result, invent adversity, or dress up something ordinary as a "hack." Don't use the startup itself as the most impressive thing; partners already know about it.

## Exercise C: reconcile the application as one account

Have each founder separately write a plain version of: what exists today; who built it and what outside help was used; who owns customer learning, product decisions and technical changes; what "customer," "user" and "revenue" mean; what happens if YC says no. Compare. A disagreement is usually a missing business decision, not a wording problem. Settle the fact before harmonizing the prose.

## A consistency failure from a published rejected application

A founder published what he said was his complete rejected 2025 application (Edicek, reported in research; not independently authenticated). One answer said the MVP "handles about 300 users," while the usage answer said only a few testers had access. A reader can't tell whether there are 300 users or none. The fix is to state technical capacity, test access and observed use as three separate facts, and to drop any "product-market fit" conclusion the behavior doesn't support. His resubmission reported 35 registered users, 6 active including the founder, 0 paying, and over 1,300 commits: a reminder to lead with registered, active and paying counts, and that commit counts are a different kind of evidence than adoption.

## A multipart question answered halfway

A founder who published his application (HOTSHOT AI, outcome unknown) noticed afterwards he'd skipped the "have you been to any YC events?" part of a multipart question. Fix: copy each question, underline every request in it, and answer each one explicitly.
