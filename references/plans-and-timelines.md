# Plans by time left

Pick the plan that matches the time before the on-time deadline. Work backward from the deadline on ycombinator.com/apply, and leave a day for founder profiles, uploads and link checks, because the form can't be submitted until every founder's profile is complete.

The founders who got in on fast, last-minute applications already had something real to report (a customer depending on a prototype, revenue from named customers). The extra time is worth most when it goes into the company, not the wording.

## Four weeks or more

1. **Week 4, evidence.** Build the fact sheet. Talk to 10 to 20 potential users about the last time the problem happened and write down what they said in their own words. Push for one commitment from someone outside the team: a payment, a pilot, data access, daily use. Ship whatever you can demo.
2. **Week 3, draft.** Fill in every answer, starting with progress and the idea questions. Write the one-liner last. Record a rough demo of the real product.
3. **Week 2, cut and test.** Send the draft to two or three readers with different jobs (one outside your field to retell it, one domain expert to find the objection, one founder close to the numbers to check them). Run the stress tests. Cut each answer, then rewrite anything that doesn't sound like you. Record the founder video from bullets.
4. **Week 1, check and submit.** Check every number against the source. Every founder completes their profile. Submit as soon as it's ready. Start the interview cheat sheet and keep the calendar open.

## Two weeks

Days 1 to 4: fact sheet, identify the weakest link, run the smallest real test that could change it (customer calls, a working feature, a pricing ask). Days 5 to 9: draft, demo, video. Days 10 to 13: reviewers, cuts, consistency check, profiles. Day 14: submit, then keep building.

## Three to six days

- **Day 1:** fact sheet, both or all founders open their profile links, and name the weakest link. Start the one test that could move it before the deadline (message every customer or user with one question, ask for a payment, ship the missing feature), since replies take a day or two.
- **Day 2:** draft every answer from the fact sheet, product and progress first. Set up demo credentials with sample data.
- **Day 3:** record the demo and the founder video from bullets. Fold in whatever the test produced.
- **Day 4 to the day before:** cut, run the stress tests and the consistency map, read every answer aloud and rewrite what doesn't sound like you, check links logged out.
- **Deadline day:** submit by midday Pacific, not at 8pm. If the test is still running, submit what's true and send an update if it lands.

## A weekend

Saturday: fact sheet in an hour, then draft every answer from it; record the demo. Sunday: cut, run the first-sentence and stranger tests, record the video, finish profiles, check links logged out, submit. If the evidence is thin, label it honestly rather than rushing a cosmetic change (a new cofounder, an inflated number).

## 48 hours or less

1. Fact sheet: product, users, revenue, team, dates. Thirty minutes.
2. Draft the one-liner, the product answer and the progress answer first; they're read first.
3. Fix the single most damaging problem (a broken demo link, a missing founder profile, an answer that doesn't say what the product does).
4. Record the video once or twice from bullets.
5. Submit on time. Label any important unknown and say what you're doing about it.

## After submitting

- Keep shipping. Progress between applying and the decision is the strongest thing a founder controls.
- Keep a dated log of what changed (users, revenue, launches, hires). It becomes the update, the interview answer to "what's changed since you applied," and the next application's before-and-after.
- Send an update only for material news, one or two at most.
- Keep the calendar open. Founders report invites with one to a few days' notice.

## Invited to interview (often 1 to 4 days' notice)

Day of invite: build the cheat sheet and the two-sentence "what are you building," every founder saying it the same way. Next day: call two or three customers so you can tell a story from this week; ship one visible improvement if you can; run one mock focused on the weakest link. Before the call: demo loaded, camera and audio tested, quiet room, backup internet.
