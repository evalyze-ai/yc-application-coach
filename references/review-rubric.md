# Review rubric and report template

This rubric grades how clearly and honestly the application shows the company. It does not predict admission, and the coach should never turn it into a probability. No public model of YC admission exists.

## Per-answer scoring (1 to 5)

| Dimension | 5 | 3 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clarity | The point is the first sentence, in plain words a stranger could repeat | The point is there but buried or wrapped in jargon | No clear answer to the question |
| Evidence | Specific, dated, checkable numbers, names or shipped things | Some specifics mixed with adjectives | Adjectives and plans only |
| Honesty | Built vs planned is clear; weaknesses stated plainly; no inflation | Some ambiguity in status or framing | Inflated, misleading or hides something asked about |
| Fit | Answers exactly what was asked, at a respectful length | Partly answers, or padded | Answers a different question |

Mark evidence "n/a" where it doesn't apply (company name, location, batch). Rewrite any answer with a dimension at 2 or below, or an average of 3 or below. When the real fix is a missing fact, say that and leave a `[NEED: ...]` instead of polishing words around the gap.

## Application-level checks

- **Story test:** write the 3-sentence story a partner would tell a colleague. If you can't, say what's missing.
- **Reader's questions** (Dalton Caldwell's list): founders and how they know each other; the idea and whether it's been heard before; what exists; evidence people want it; seriousness; something special. Mark each strong, weak or missing.
- **Weakest link:** the one concern most likely to sink it, and whether it's a company, evidence or communication problem.
- **Consistency:** numbers, dates, roles and descriptions match across all answers, the video, the site and any deck.
- **Completeness:** every founder profile filled; demo attached; links and credentials work; every field answered.
- **Voice:** reads like the founder; no AI tells (see the list in SKILL.md).

## Two-part verdict (never a probability)

Writing and company are judged separately, because they fail separately and often together.

- **Writing:** Ready, or Needs these fixes (list them; all doable before the deadline).
- **Company risk:** the weakest link, whether it's a company, evidence or communication problem, and the fastest real test. If it's a company problem that can't be fixed before the deadline, say whether to apply anyway (usually yes, since applying costs little) and what to fix before the next batch.

For a readiness question with no draft to grade, skip the writing line and answer apply-now-or-not directly.
