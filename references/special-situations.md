# Special situations

None of these disqualifies a founder on its own. In nearly every case YC's answer is to apply as you are and sort out the mechanics after acceptance. What changes is which answers carry the most weight. For how to word the uncomfortable facts themselves (no revenue, a cofounder who left, a long time with modest progress, an agency build, a recent pivot and more), see `hard-answers.md`.

| Situation | What YC says (FAQ unless noted) | What to do on the application |
|---|---|---|
| Solo founder | Regularly accepted; one-person startups are tough and more likely to succeed with a cofounder. YC said in Sept 2026 nearly one in five of its companies has a solo founder | Answer the cofounder question in a sentence or two. Show how building and selling are both covered and show unusual speed. Never invent a cofounder. Some solo founders were told to find a cofounder and later got in solo after raising money or hiring |
| No technical founder | The team must be able to build the product; for most businesses that means a technical cofounder | The hardest case. Name exactly who built what and who owns the code. With an agency, get answers in writing to: who owns the code and IP, whether the agency holds or is owed equity, whether there's an ongoing retainer, and who fixes a production bug tomorrow. Show something a founder personally shipped. Start a real search for a technical cofounder (YC Co-Founder Matching). Don't present an agency build as in-house |
| Employed or in school | You can apply; founders must work full-time during and after the batch if accepted | State each founder's status and quit or leave date. Students who want to finish first can use Early Decision |
| Outside the US | No need to incorporate, get visas or licenses before applying. YC invests in US, Canada, Cayman and Singapore corporations; others create a parent company after acceptance. YC connects founders with immigration attorneys | State current location and entity plainly. Commit clearly to the in-person batch in San Francisco. Start visa planning when invited to interview |
| Already raised money | Many batch companies have raised over $1M; previous raises at higher valuations aren't a barrier | Disclose every round with instrument, amount, cap and investors. Say if you're raising now |
| Already earning revenue | 7% of recent batches had over $50K monthly revenue at acceptance | Lead with monthly revenue and retention. Explain why YC is useful now |
| Did another accelerator | Allowed, but YC may expect more progress | Disclose it and show what happened since |
| A YC company does something similar | Not a factor in decisions | Focus on your read of the problem and what's different |
| Pivoting, or several ideas | One application per batch; list alternatives in "other ideas" | Apply with your favorite idea. If you pivot before the interview, send an update and bring evidence for the new direction |
| Cofounders haven't met in person | The form asks directly | Say so, how long you've worked together remotely, and when you'll meet |
| Studio, agency, consulting or several ventures | No public rule found | Make it obvious which company is applying, who works on it full-time, who owns the IP and code, where revenue comes from, and what happens to the other commitments |
| Repeat founder or experienced operator | No age or experience criteria | State past outcomes exactly. Lead with the domain insight and how fast you're moving now |
| Young founder | No age criteria | Lead with shipped work: users, downloads, repos, results |
| Hardware, bio, defense, deep tech | YC funds any field; recent batches include more physical and hard tech | Lead with technical milestones (what works, tested how, by whom), the first customer or pilot, and the next de-risking step. Have the hardware on camera in the interview |
| "Capital intensive" concern | No public YC rule; founders report it as a stated reason for passing on hardware | Answer with unit economics: bill of materials, price, gross margin, install time and who installs, cash needed per 10 or 100 deployments, and whether customers prepay, lease or pay as a service. Price against the labor or cost the product replaces, which often fixes a "market looks small" read at the same time |
| Health, finance or other regulated data | No public YC rule | One or two lines on data handling: agreements with customers and any model providers, what's compliant today versus planned (labeled honestly). Never show real patient or customer data in a demo |
| Consumer | Funded, smaller share of recent batches | Lead with retention and engagement, not downloads |
| Big equity gap between founders | Partners have said YC's software flags large gaps for attention | Explain it in one sentence that holds up, or fix it on its merits before applying. Don't change a cap table cosmetically |
| Applying late | Late applications are considered, without a promised reply date; late status can't be changed | Apply anyway if it's ready. Founders have been accepted on applications submitted after the deadline |
| Rejected before | About half of a typical batch applied more than once | See `reapply-update-rejection.md` |

## Tactics to refuse

If a founder asks for help with any of these, say no and explain why:
- Reapplying to the same batch from a second account (one application per batch; YC keeps applications).
- Listing San Francisco as where you live after a short visit.
- Adding a nominal cofounder for optics.
- Inflating numbers, annualizing silently, or presenting LOIs, unpaid pilots, signed-but-uncollected contracts or pipeline as revenue. (Collected money from paid pilots is real one-time revenue; label it as such.)
- Writing a video script for founders to read.
- Paying anyone who promises to get the founder in. Dalton Caldwell has warned about outside parties claiming to influence admissions.
- Collecting recommendations from YC founders who don't know the team. YC says introductions aren't needed, and a recommendation from someone who doesn't know you adds little.
- Reshaping the idea to match the Requests for Startups. The RFS page says the requests are a fraction of what YC funds. If the company fits one, a line saying so is fine; never present it as better odds.
